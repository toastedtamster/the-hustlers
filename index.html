Project Direction

Project Goal & Scope

A clear statement of what our project is intended to accomplish and what is intentionally outside the project scope.

Project Goal

Our goal is to help Maldora Beauty streamline its booking system, and we'll know we've succeeded when its clients are more satisfied with their booking experience - and the solution should also works for other service providers who face the same problem.

Out of Scope

  • We are not handling any payment transactions.
  • We are not building/coding a software application completely from scratch.
  • We are not dealing with data storage and client security.

Shared Purpose

Goal: Our shared goal for the project is to improve the business, make operations more efficient, and learn and improve technical skills with hands-on experience.

What success looks like: Success for the team means that the team successfully designed an efficient team for users.

Communication

Platform: iMessage

Response time: Less than 24 hours on both weekdays and weekends.

Meetings: Preferably Sunday afternoons (after 4 PM) biweekly or once a weekly when necessary.

Decision-Making

How decisions are made: We will try to communicate and have everyone aligned before making a significant decision. We can also take a majority vote and come to a consensus.

How disagreements are handled: The process will be communicating directly with the individual where the disagreement arises first.

Working Agreements

Participation: Everyone showing up to meetings, contributing to discussions, sharing their thoughts, and completing the tasks on time.

Accountability: We will do check-ins and meetings ahead of time (before a deadline) to ensure everyone is on track.

Deadlines: We will set internal deadlines on when the team would like to complete tasks. The timing depends on the task, but the timing would typically be 1 week beforehand.

Conflict Resolution

Unresponsive teammate: The team will first send a direct message, but after the dedicated response time, we will send emails to their official school account. After that, we may contact the instructor if the teammate is unresponsive for longer periods of time.

How concerns are raised: Team members can raise their concerns in meetings and class sessions. Team members can also notify others of their concerns through text messages (our primary communication).

When to involve the instructor: Situations where team members have unprofessional disagreements or when members have discussed an issue but the issue continues persisting warrant bringing in the instructor.

Success Metrics

What will make us proud: Seeing the product work and having a simple service that can be translated across businesses.

Behaviors that define success: Holding each other accountable and making sure we all communicate.

Client Overview

Client

Theodora Eremionkhale

Maldora Beauty

What makes them a good client?

A small business that does not have a booking system or a website. We thought that this client would be a good start both because both the client and our group would be building a product from the start. Some of us have experience with working with beauty supplies, and others have a part-time job at a spa with an established system.

How did the first conversation go?

The client expressed their business needs and challenges. They talked about how their business started, and it was interesting and inspiring.

Why are you excited to work with this client?

We felt it would be interesting and fun to build a product for this client, one that can also be introduced to other service-based businesses.

Client Interview Outline

Meeting 1 with Client Recap · September 13, 2026

Current Booking Process

1 · Google FormCollects contact information, hairstyle, and location.
2 · Client ReviewThe owner checks availability and whether she can commute to the location.
3 · ConfirmationThe owner manually sends a confirmation email that includes policies.
4 · AppointmentThe client receives service at the confirmed appointment time.
View Interview Questions 13 Questions
1. What inspired you to start the business?

The owner wanted a side income during college and decided to turn her braiding skills into a business.

2. Do you eventually want to scale the business?

The owner is interested in scaling the business. She mentioned an interest in TV, film, and events and is currently exploring that area. She has assisted other hairstylists on set for films and photoshoots and would like to do this long term.

3. What are some characteristics of your current clientele?

The business primarily serves women ages 18–30, especially college students and Black women with textured hair.

4. Can you walk us through the booking process?

Previous process: Client sends a DM showing interest → mini consultation for hairstyle, location, and time → confirmation through Instagram → appointment.

Current process: Google Form → owner reviews availability and commute feasibility → confirmation email.

5. How do clients confirm their booking? Is there a deposit?

The owner does not require a deposit. She manually sends an email confirmation that includes policies, which can take time when she is at her job.

6. Where does the booking process slow down?

The Google Form previously required clients to upload a photo of their hair, which required signing into a Google account. The owner believed this may have discouraged some clients, so the photo-upload section was removed. She also increased prices around the same time and is unsure which factor contributed to the slowdown.

7. Have you tried another solution for booking?

She has considered a website and chose Google Forms because it is free and easy to set up. She previously tried Booksy but had difficulty accessing money paid through the site, and clients could book during times when she was unavailable.

8. Have clients ever been double-booked?

Appointments sometimes run over into another client's appointment time. When this happens, the owner and her sister split up so the other person can begin the next appointment.

9. How do you handle dissatisfaction or miscommunication?

When clients are dissatisfied, she may schedule an appointment to fix the issue. If the problem is hairstyle maintenance, she directs clients to resources. She has also dealt with clients trying to request a refund after receiving the full service, which influenced her use of cash or Zelle instead of third-party systems.

10. What are the most frustrating booking challenges?

Miscommunication is a major challenge. Some clients request appointments beyond the stated 10-mile service area from downtown Los Angeles, while others refer to older Instagram pricing and expect to pay outdated rates.

11. How is returning-client information stored?

For recurring clients, she remembers some details and often goes back to Instagram chats. Google Forms also populates client information into a Google Sheet.

12. Are there privacy concerns with client information?

Yes. Client information is stored on Instagram, which raises concerns about hackers. Password management is also a concern, and the spreadsheet containing client information does not have a password.

13. How do transportation limits affect booking?

The owner communicates the distance she can travel. If a client is outside that range, the client may cover transportation or Uber costs. She has also increased prices to cover transportation costs.

Client Needs

  • Keeping client information safe.
  • Automation, including automated messages and confirmation messages.
  • Having rates and the price list on the same page as the booking form to reduce pricing misunderstandings.

Additional Notes

  • The client values Instagram because it helps her confirm that a customer appears to be a real person and gives her a sense of safety when vetting clients.
  • Nontanee recommended Square as a possible booking system.
  • The team noted cybersecurity practices such as multifactor authentication, stronger passwords, and Bitwarden as possible areas to discuss with the client.
